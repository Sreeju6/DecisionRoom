01
INTRODUCTION
The Government of Karnataka has formulated the 
Karnataka Startup Policy 2025-2030 with a vision 
to position the state as a global leader in innovation 
and entrepreneurship. Karnataka continues to 
play a pivotal role in stimulating the startup 
environment at both national and international 
levels, leveraging its tech-enabled ecosystem, 
strong talent base, and entrepreneurial strength.
Karnataka is strategically positioned to emerge as 
a global powerhouse in the startup ecosystem, 
fostering an environment where entrepreneurs 
can scale, innovate, and capitalize on diverse 
opportunities. With its robust infrastructure, 
progressive policies, and thriving talent pool, the 
state continues to attract high-growth startups, 
DeepTech ventures, and disruptive innovations 
across multiple sectors.
The Startup Policy 2025 aims to strengthen 
Karnataka’s position as the ‘Champion State’ for 
startups, adopting a holistic approach to 
ecosystem development through strategic 
interventions across key pillars such as Funding 
and Grants, Market Access and Expansion, 
Infrastructure & Tech Development Support, 
Talent & Skill Development and Social Inclusion. 
The policy guarantees financial assistance for 
startups by offering marketing cost support, 
enabling them to expand operations, enhance 
visibility, and accelerate growth in competitive 
markets.
Recognizing Bengaluru’s global leadership in the 
startup ecosystem, the government is committed 
to maintain its top position while simultaneously 
developing emerging cities Beyond Bengaluru as 
preferred startup destinations. This policy focuses 
on expanding innovation, fostering emerging-tech 
entrepreneurship and strengthening industry 
collaborations to create a robust infrastructure 
and a conducive policy environment for startups 
to thrive.
The global startup ecosystem continues to 
expand, with over 150 million startups worldwide. 
This rapid growth highlights the increasing role of 
entrepreneurship in economic development, job 
creation, and technological innovation.
According to the Global Startup Ecosystem Report 
2025 by Startup Genome, the top 5 global startup 
ecosystems are: Silicon Valley, New York City, 
London, Tel Aviv, and Boston & Beijing. The global 
startup ecosystem in 2024–2025 has experienced 
robust growth, fueled by technological innovation 
and significant Venture Capital (VC) investments. 
The Global VC investment rose to $368.3 billion 
across 35,684 deals in 2024, up from $349.4 
billion in 2023. The United States led with $209 
billion in VC investments, marking its third-highest 
total in 20 years. Despite the regional disparities, 
the overall increase in global VC funding 
underscores the resilience and dynamism of the 
startup landscape.
The global startup ecosystem is undergoing rapid 
transformation, serving as a catalyst for economic 
growth, technological innovation, and 
entrepreneurial development. As startups adopt 
AI, DeepTech, blockchain, and sustainability-
driven models, they benefit from increased cross-
border collaborations, venture capital investments, 
and government-backed incubators. This 
accelerated support will help in fueling the rise of 
next-generation unicorns and decacorns, driving 
global innovation.

India has rapidly evolved into a global startup 
powerhouse, ranking as the third-largest startup 
ecosystem in the world with over 1.59 lakhs 
startups as recognized by Department for 
Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), 
of which 73,151 are women led as of 2025. These 
startups have supported employment generation 
to the tune of 16.6 lakhs.
India’s startup ecosystem has witnessed 
remarkable expansion, growing from ~ 500 
startups in 2016 to 199,979 in 2025, reflecting a 
39,895.8% surge. Over 400+ incubators and over 
100 unicorns in sectors such as fintech, edtech, 
health-tech, and e-commerce continue to drive 
innovation and investment in the country. While 
Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Mumbai, and Delhi-NCR 
remain key startup hubs, 45% of startups now 
originate from non-metro cities. This reflects the 
broadening entrepreneurial landscape supported 
by initiatives like Startup India Seed Fund Scheme 
and Atal Incubation Centers (AICs) that provide 
funding and mentorship to startups.
The startup ecosystem is undergoing a significant 
transformation, backed by advancements in 
emerging technologies. Generative AI and 
DeepTech is drawing substantial investments, 
reinforcing India’s status as a global innovation 
leader. With 5 million software developers, India 
stands as the second-largest developer nation, 
driving technological progress. E-commerce and 
Fintech remain at the forefront of funding activity, 
while the rise of quick commerce is reshaping 
consumer behavior and accelerating digital 
transactions.
India’s startup ecosystem is fueled by continuous 
technological advancements that ensure 
sustained growth and innovation. According to a 
report released by CII in 2024, the startup and 
venture economy could potentially contribute ~$1 
trillion to India’s estimated $7 trillion economy by 
2030 and create 50 million jobs.7 India is well-
positioned to lead the future of startups on the 
world stage with flagship initiatives such as Make 
in India, Startup India, Stand Up India, SAMRIDH, 
Aspire, Atal Innovation Mission, Startup India 
Showcase, and Startup India Innovation Week. 
These efforts reinforce and further establish the 
country as a global powerhouse in 
entrepreneurship, driving innovation and sustained 
economic growth.
Karnataka stands as the unrivaled leader in India’s 
startup landscape, playing a pivotal role in 
positioning the country as a global innovation and 
entrepreneurial hub. As of 2025, the state is home 
to more than 18,952 registered startups, as per 
DPIIT.  
Karnataka stands at the forefront of India’s 
innovation-driven growth, with a thriving startup 
ecosystem anchored in Bengaluru and expanding 
rapidly across Beyond Bengaluru regions. The 
state is emerging as a national leader in DeepTech, 
Artificial Intelligence (AI), and Quantum 
Technologies, supported by world-class 
institutions and forward-looking policies. 
Initiatives like INNOVERSE, a first-of- its-kind 
DeepTech incubation and innovation center are

its international standing. It leads among Asian 
cities featured in the top 20, with New Delhi and

innovation powerhouse and reinforces its position 
as India’s premier startup ecosystem.

and accelerate groundbreaking innovations.

strengthen its global reputation as a technology 
powerhouse and a launchpad for unicorns and

forward.
As a state, Karnataka is open, future-ready and 
grounded in inclusive innovation.

02
POLICY RATIONAL
i. To position the state as a global innovation hub, fostering a vibrant and supportive ecosystem for 
nurturing startups throughout their lifecycle. 
ii. To provide resources, mentorship, and infrastructure desired to drive sustainable growth and 
technological advancements.
i. Through this policy Karnataka seeks to establish up to 25,000 additional startups, out of 
which upto 10,000 startups emerging from clusters Beyond Bengaluru. By expanding the 
entrepreneurial footprints, the initiative helps the state to enhance its role as a leading 
innovation hub. 
ii. Encourage innovation in Artificial Intelligence, blockchain, quantum computing, and other 
deep- tech fields to position the state as a leader in emerging and advanced technologies. 
iii. Expand innovation hubs, incubation centers, and co-working spaces to support entrepreneurs 
across different regions. 
iv. Strengthen international collaborations, trade partnerships, and global networking 
opportunities for startups to expand their reach. 
v. Enable investment opportunities for startups by facilitating access to institutional and angel 
investors while leveraging government-backed financial support. 
vi. Support the development of incubation and acceleration facilities to serve as key enablers in 
strengthening the startup ecosystem. 
vii. Facilitate innovative solutions in social governance sectors, leveraging the ESG framework to 
solve pressing environmental and social issues while aligning with Sustainable Development 
Goals (SDGs). 
viii. Encourage solutions that address social challenges by integrating impact-driven business 
models for boosting social entrepreneurship.

The startup journey is marked by distinct stages 
each with its own challenges, opportunities, and 
critical decisions. To foster sustainable growth 
and innovation, tailored support mechanisms are 
essential at every phase. From the spark of an 
idea in the seed stage to international expansion 
and IPO readiness in the late expansion phase, 
startups require a well curated combination of 
support such as funding, mentorship, 
infrastructure, and market access. The following 
breakdown outlines the type of support available 
across the startup lifecycle, ensuring founders 
have the tools and guidance needed to navigate 
and thrive in this dynamic ecosystem  
 z Stage 1: Seed Stage:
• Ideation Grants / Grant-in-Aid:  Non-equity 
funding to build MVPs or conduct feasibility 
studies.
• Mentoring & Coaching: Guidance on business 
model, product-market fit, and team building.
• Incubation Support:  Access to co-working 
spaces, basic infrastructure, and 
administrative support.
• Technical Support:  Help with prototyping, 
product design, and early tech stack 
decisions.
• Networking Events:  Exposure to other 
founders, early-stage investors, and domain 
experts.
• Legal & IP Support: Assistance with company 
registration, IP filing, and compliance basics  
 z Stage 2: Early Stage: 
• Seed Funding / Angel Investment:  Initial 
capital to build and launch the product.
• Accelerator Programs: Structured programs 
offering mentorship, funding, and demo days.
• Preferential Market Access:  Help with pilot 
projects, early adopters, and B2B/B2G 
connections.
• Business Development Mentoring:  Sales 
strategy, pricing models, and customer 
feedback loops.
• Internship & Talent Access: Connecting with 
students and professionals for hiring.
• Branding & Marketing Support:  Guidance on 
digital marketing, PR, and go-to-market 
strategy.
  Stage 3: Growth Stage:
• Venture Capital Funding  for scaling teams, 
marketing, and operations.
• Advanced Mentoring: Strategic guidance on 
scaling, leadership, and international 
expansion.
• Market Expansion Support:  Entry into new 
geographies or sectors, export readiness 
(Including international)
• Technology Upgradation Support:  
Automation tools, booster support
• Corporate Partnerships:  Collaborations with 
large enterprises for distribution or co- 
development.
• Regulatory & Compliance Advisory: Help 
with sector-specific regulations
  Stage 4: Late Expansion:
• Private Equity / Strategic Investment:  
Support for large-scale funding for 
acquisitions or global expansion.
• IPO Readiness Support:  Legal, financial, and 
operational preparation for public listing.
• Global Market Access:  Trade missions, 
international expos, and cross-border 
partnerships.
• Advanced Mentoring:  M&A Advisory (for 
mergers, acquisitions, or strategic 
partnerships); Corporate governance and risk 
management
To ensure its offerings align with the 
evolving needs of startups, the Karnataka 
Startup Policy has modularized its 
programs and initiatives in coordination 
with the stages of the startup lifecycle. 
Government aims to strengthen 
Karnataka’s startup ecosystem through 7 
essential pillars for implementing targeted 
initiatives to foster innovation, growth, and 
sustainability. Each of the Seven policy 
pillars is designed to provide targeted 
support at key inflection points:

i. Pillar 1: Funding and Grants:  A vital pillar 
that provides financial backing for early-
stage startups, offering grants, funding, 
and subsidies to foster innovation, 
scalability, and long-term sustainability. It 
ensures entrepreneurs have access to 
essential resources, enabling them to 
develop pioneering ideas and drive 
business growth.
ii. Pillar 2: Incubation and Infrastructure: 
Essential in fostering a thriving startup 
ecosystem. Karnataka shall invest in 
state-of-the-art incubation centers, Centre 
of Excellence and R&D hubs to provide 
startups with essential resources and 
support. Additionally, initiatives such as 
public-private partnerships under this 
pillar, shall drive innovation by enabling 
startups to access cutting-edge 
technology and industry expertise.
iii. Pillar 3: Mentoring and Skill Development: 
Cornerstone for fostering innovation and 
entrepreneurship in Karnataka’s startup 
ecosystem. Funding for specialized 
training program, subsidies for industry 
certification, grants for startup-focused 
skilling initiatives, incubation-linked 
training grants and mentorship-driven 
financial support shall help nurture the 
next generation startup leaders.
iv. Pillar 4: Market Access and Expansion: 
Key for startups aiming to scale and enter 
new regions. The state shall facilitate 
global partnerships, government 
procurement incentives that support 
startups to reach wider audiences and 
strengthen their market presence.
v. Pillar 5: International Collaboration and 
access: It promotes global engagement 
by facilitating international partnerships, 
cross-border innovation, and access to 
overseas markets. Through targeted 
strategic initiatives, startups gain exposure 
to global ecosystems, mentorship, and go-
to-market support. These efforts aim to 
position Karnataka-based startups for 
international competitiveness, attract 
foreign investment, and foster knowledge 
exchange across borders.
vi. Pillar 6: Inclusion and Sustainability: This 
shall encourage startups and young 
entrepreneurs to leverage disruptive 
technologies and innovative business 
models to address pressing social impact 
challenges. Tailored funding programs, 
grants for women-led and rural ventures, 
and support for grassroots 
entrepreneurship shall foster inclusivity 
and drive community-driven innovation.
vii. Pillar 7: Regulatory Facilitation: This aims 
to streamline regulatory processes for 
startups by promoting simplified 
compliance, single-window clearances, 
and proactive engagement with relevant 
authorities. Through targeted measures, 
Karnataka seeks to create a business-
friendly environment that reduces 
bureaucratic hurdles and accelerates 
startup growth.

POLICY PILLARS
Launched in 2017, the ELEVATE program by the 
Government of Karnataka, is a flagship program 
designed to support and fund idea to PoC stage 
innovative startups in the state. ELEVATE creates 
a thriving entrepreneurial environment by 
facilitating mentorship, incubation, legal aid, and 
connections to investors. This initiative facilitates 
seamless integration with Department supported 
K-Tech Hubs including incubation and 
acceleration centers and Centers of Excellence 
(CoEs) to provide startups with access to 
mentorship, incubation and ecosystem 
integration.
This holistic support system not only fosters 
innovation but also contributes to job creation 
and talent development, solidifying Karnataka’s 
reputation as a dynamic startup hub.
The government is committed to fostering 
entrepreneurship and driving innovation through 
dedicated funding support:
• A one-time Grant-in-Aid of up to INR 50 Lakhs 
is being provided for startups in need of 
early-stage seed funding to transform their 
ideas into a proof-of-concept.
• A greater focus will continue to be given to 
emerging sectors, by allocating 10% of the 
Elevate Grant-In-Aid Scheme for startups 
working in Deep Tech, Assistive Technology 
and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 
including Circular Economy, Renewable 
Energy / Sustainability / Climate Change.
• Recognized startups under this scheme will 
continue to receive mentoring and incubation 
support at subsidized rates from government-
backed incubation centers, assisting them in 
product development and validation.
The Department of E, IT & Bt, along with the 
Department of Social Welfare, supports an 
initiative to empower SC and ST entrepreneurs by 
identifying and supporting startups under the 
Scheduled Caste Sub Plan (SCSP) and Tribal Sub 
Plan (TSP) schemes. This program is designed to 
provide comprehensive support across various 
stages of the startup lifecycle, ensuring equitable 
opportunities for entrepreneurs from marginalized 
communities.
The program aims to reduce barriers, enhance 
competitiveness, and drive economic 
empowerment for SC and ST entrepreneurs, 
contributing to a more inclusive startup landscape 
in Karnataka. The approach fosters long-term 
economic empowerment by enabling sustainable 
and scalable ventures. It plays a crucial role in 
building a more inclusive and representative 
startup ecosystem in Karnataka, where innovation 
thrives across all social and economic 
backgrounds.
Through this initiative, selected startups will 
receive grant-in-aid support of up to INR 50 lakhs, 
enabling them to develop and scale their 
businesses effectively. In addition to financial 
assistance, the program offers mentoring, 
incubation support, market access, and other 
essential enablers to foster sustainable growth.

• The Rajiv Gandhi Entrepreneurial Program 
aims to nurture early-stage innovators, 
especially recent graduates, through financial 
support and structured mentorship. RGEP 
helps transform raw ideas into viable 
prototypes, encouraging a shift from job-
seeking to job-creating mindsets. This 
initiative strengthens innovation and 
contributes to a more vibrant, youth-driven 
entrepreneurial landscape in the state.
• RGEP provides early-stage funding support, 
offering a monthly stipend of INR 25,000 for 
12 months to selected entrepreneurs. The 
program aims to help them develop 
prototypes, minimum viable products 
(MVPs), or proofs of concept, reducing 
financial barriers and encouraging new 
business creation. RGEP seeks to shift young 
professionals away from traditional job-
seeking toward innovation-driven enterprises.
• Entrepreneurs selected under this program 
receive mentorship and incubation support 
at government-backed Innovation Hubs, 
ensuring structured guidance throughout 
their 12-month journey.
• This ensures that only the most promising 
ideas receive support, helping Karnataka 
build a strong foundation for cutting-edge 
businesses in sectors such as technology, 
sustainability, and assistive solutions.
The government recognizes the significant role 
of startups in advancing the circular economy, 
improving resource efficiency through reuse, 
recycling, and refurbishment of natural resources. 
By driving demand for sustainability-focused 
products, startups contribute to a more resilient 
and eco-conscious market. To connect innovative 
business technology solutions and address 
demanding Environmental, Social, and 
Governance (ESG) challenges, key government 
department such as Urban Development, 
Transport, Energy, Agriculture, Ecology & 
Environment, Animal Husbandry, Revenue, 
Health, and Women & Child Welfare will work 
collaboratively to identify societal issues that 
startups can solve through technology-driven 
interventions.  
Additionally, Smart Governance challenges will 
be introduced in partnership with ‘Center for 
e-Governance, DPAR’ aiming to enhance 
government service delivery and operational 
efficiency by integrating cutting- edge innovations 
from emerging startups.
• Every year, up to three challenges shall be 
launched with collaborative funding from 
Government Departments and Corporates, 
providing startups with an opportunity to 
develop innovative solutions. The winners 
will be receiving an initial grant of INR 10 
Lakhs to test and demonstrate their ideas, 
followed by additional funding of INR 50 
Lakhs for full-scale deployment, ensuring 
impactful and sustainable implementation.
• The Department of E, IT and Bt, in collaboration 
with other Government Departments, aims to 
promote CSR investments under the 
Companies Act in projects aligned with 
Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). 
These include Renewable Energy, 
Sustainability, and Climate Change 
technology-driven solutions that create long-
term transformative value and drive 
sustainable growth. To support startups, the 
same funding pattern as previous initiatives 
will be adopted.
• By bridging the gap between public 
challenges and startup innovation, GCK 
fosters a culture of problem-solving 
entrepreneurship and strengthens 
Karnataka’s position as a hub for mission-
driven startups.
The Karnataka Information Technology Venture 
Capital Fund (KITVEN Fund) serves as the nodal 
agency managing a dedicated venture capital 
fund of INR 100 crores aimed at nurturing high-
potential startups in emerging and deep tech 
sectors. The fund will support innovation across 
domains such as Artificial Intelligence, Machine 
Learning, Electric Vehicles, MedTech, Robotics, 
Drones, and other transformative technologies.

The government is committed to fostering the

provide sustained financial support to startups

high-impact projects.

as, but not limited to AI, Biotechnology, Quantum 
Computing, Advanced Materials, Climate tech, 
Space tech, etc.
A significant amount has been allocated over the 
next five years to support call for applications, 
ensuring at least one call per year. The funding

critical financial support, the program seeks to

ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA, 
POLICY VALIDITY AND 
SANCTION PROCESS
To avail the policy incentives and concessions, the entity 
should meet the definition of Startup as per the Startup 
Policy and must be registered with Karnataka Startup Cell 
under Karnataka Innovation & Technology Society (KITS), 
Department of E, IT and Bt, GoK as a Startup.
A. Definitions of Startups
As per Gazette Notification No. G.S.R. 108(E), dated 
February 04, 2026.
In this notification, —
a. ‘Startup’ means an entity which:
i. is incorporated or registered in India as a private 
limited company (as defined in the Companies Act, 
2013) or registered as a partnership firm (registered 
under section 59 of the Partnership Act, 1932) or a 
limited liability partnership (under the Limited Liability 
Partnership Act, 2008) or a Multi-State Cooperative 
Society registered with the Central Registrar of 
Cooperative Societies (under the Multi-State 
Cooperative Societies Act, 2002) or a Cooperative 
Society registered under any State or Union Territory 
Cooperative Societies Act with the respective Registrar 
 of Cooperative Societies in India;    
ii. is within a period of ten years from the date of its 
incorporation or registration;  
iii. has a turnover for any of the financial years since 
incorporation or registration not exceeding two 
hundred crore rupees; and  
iv. is working towards innovation, development or 
improvement of products or processes or services, or 
is a scalable business model with a high potential of 
employment generation or wealth creation.
Deeptech Startup Definition:
Provided that, in the case of an entity recognised as a ‘Deep 
Tech Startup’ under this notification:
a. the period specified in clause (ii) shall be up to twenty 
years from the date of its incorporation or registration; 
and
b. the turnover limit specified in clause (iii) shall be three 
hundred crore rupees for any of the financial years 
since incorporation or registration.
Provided further that,  an entity formed by splitting up or 
reconstruction of an existing business shall not be 
considered a Startup.  
Explanation-
For the purposes of this notification, an entity shall cease 
to be a Startup on completion of ten years from the date of 
its incorporation or registration, or if its turnover for any 
previous year exceeds two hundred crore rupees.
Provided that, in the case of an entity recognised as a Deep 
Tech Startup under this notification, such entity shall cease 
to be a Deep Tech Startup on completion of twenty years 
from the date of its incorporation or registration, or if its 
turnover for any previous year exceeds three hundred
crore rupees.
B. Exit Clause
A startup will cease to receive benefits under this policy on 
completion of ten years from the date of its incorporation/ 
registration or if its turnover for any previous year exceeds 
INR 200 crores.
Benefits and offerings under this policy shall be 
extended periodically through dedicated committees 
constituted for specific purposes. The Operational 
Guidelines of this policy shall outline the detailed 
modalities, eligibility criteria, and processes for 
availing such benefits.
All schemes and incentives will be administered 
subject to budgetary provisions and availability of 
funds during the policy period.

DEFINITIONS
1. Acceleration Programs: Acceleration programs are short to medium term mentoring programs to 
support startups with business expansion once they have scaled up from an idea to formal product 
launch in the market. Startups typically enter accelerator program for a fixed period of time as part 
of a cohort of companies
2. Accelerator: An accelerator is typically a three-to-four-month program for startups at early traction 
and scaling stage. It is a process of intense, rapid, and immersive education aimed at accelerating 
the growth of the startup. The accelerator may take a set amount of seed equity from the startups 
in their cohorts in exchange for capital and mentorship.
3. Angel Investors: Individual wealthy private investor who provide early-stage seed funding to small 
startups or entrepreneurs. The Angel investor should be registered with SEBI or banks or GoK/GoI 
approved incubation centers.
4. Assistive Technology: Assistive technology (AT) is any item, piece of equipment, software program, 
product system, or service that is used to increase, maintain, or improve the functional capabilities 
of persons with disabilities or older people, or people with gradual functional decline as defined by 
WHO, thereby promoting their well-being.
5. Beyond Bengaluru: Used to describe cities/areas in Karnataka outside Bengaluru (Urban) District
6. Circular Economy Startups: Startups who qualify to be part of Circular Economy Startups Innovation 
are defined as those startups that have solutions contributing to circular economy model which 
involves sharing, leasing, reusing, repairing, refurbishing, reducing and recycling of existing materials 
and products as long as possible. The technology enabled innovative solution should be aligned to 
the principles of circular economy to tackles challenges like climate change, biodiversity loss, waste, 
and pollution etc.
7. DeepTech:  
a. To understand a deep tech startup, we must first understand what a deep tech startup isn’t, then 
identify parameters that define a deep tech startup. A non-deep tech startup typically relies 
predominantly on the business model as its differentiating factor or moat, rather than science and 
technology as the underlying competitive edge. It is based on easily producible or replicable 
technologies without significant advancements.  
b. A deep tech startup involves early-stage technologies based on scientific or engineering 
advancements, which are yet to be developed for any commercial applications.  
c. A deep tech startup typically produces a solution along an unexplored pathway based on new 
knowledge within a scientific or engineering discipline or by combining knowledge from multiple 
disciplines.  
d. The above two factors enable deep tech startups to create and own Intellectual Property (IP), 
which is its distinguishing parameter from non-deep tech startups  
e. A deep tech startup carries a large technical or scientific uncertainty and hence presents a large 
opportunity or risk depending upon whether it succeeds.


--- PDF PAGE 33 ---

f. A deep tech startup is characterized by extended development timelines and requires high capital 
intensity.  
g. Complexity: They have complex technical solutions that have the potential to redefine or create 
new markets  
h. At the Intersection of Technologies: They may leverage more than one technology to solve 
problems.
Note: The definition of a startup as outlined in the Karnataka Startup Policy will be applicable for the 
ELEVATE X grant-in-aid scheme.
8. Emerging Technologies:  Emerging technologies are new technologies whose development or 
practical applications, or both are still largely inherent, such that they are emerging into eminence 
from a background of nonexistence or ambiguity. Emerging technologies are often anticipated as 
capable of changing the status quo.
9. Entity: A Private Limited Company (as per the Companies Act 2013), a Registered Partnership Firm 
(under the Partnership Act, 1932) or Limited Liability Partnership (under the Limited Liability 
Partnership Act, 2008)
10. Grassroot Innovation or Innovation with Rural Impact:  Startups who qualify to be part of Grassroot 
or Rural Innovations are defined as those startups that lead bottom-up solutions for sustainable 
development; solutions that respond to local situation / interest / values of rural communities and are 
aimed at improving the socio-economic condition/ ease of living and standard of living of the rural 
communities
11. Incubator:  
a. New Incubator: An incubator should have a minimum 7,500 sq ft area and should be an incubation 
facility for a minimum 50 seats. The incubator should have provision to provide necessary 
infrastructure to startups such as plug & play facilities, meeting/conference rooms/office space or 
shared services, high-speed internet access, etc. The Incubator shall also provide various services to 
startups such as mentors, training, funding, legal services, accountancy services, technical assistance, 
networking activities, marketing assistance etc.  
b. Expansion/ Upgradation of Incubator: An incubator increasing its capacity by additional 2,500 sq 
ft area, and incubation facility for a minimum 25 additional seats or more.
12. Innovation Regulatory Sandbox:  An innovation regulatory sandbox is a regulatory approach, 
customarily epitomized in writing and published, that allows conscious, time bound testing of 
innovations under a regulator’s oversight
13. K-Tech Ecosystem:  The State boasts of a vast tech ecosystem comprising Innovation Hubs, 
Incubators, Centres of Excellence, Technology & Business Incubators, Common Instrumentation 
Facilities, NAIN Institutions etc.
14. New Age Innovation Network (NAIN) 2.0:  New Age Innovation Network (NAIN) centres established 
by Government under the Startup Policy 2022.
15. Reimbursement: Compensation given by the State/ public body/ organization for a defined expense 
by giving them an amount equal to what was agreed upon.
16. Renewable Energy, Climate change, Sustainability Startups:  Startups who qualify to be part of 
Renewable Energy, Climate change, Sustainability startups Innovation are defined as those startups 
with innovative technology solutions addressing critical innovation gaps in the Renewable Energy,

Climate change, Sustainability Innovation sector.  
17. Sandbox Operator: The Sandbox Operator under the Karnataka Innovation Authority Act is defined as 
‘An agency or instrumentality of the Government of Karnataka, notified by the Authority under Section 
13 of KIA Act to oversee and implement a Regulatory Sandbox.
18. Seed Funding: Seed capital is the initial funding used to begin the creation of a business or a new 
product. Seed capital is used to develop a business idea to the point that it can be presented effectively 
to venture capital firms that have large amounts of money to invest.
19. Subsidy: Subsidy is a sum of money granted by the state or a public body to help an industry or business 
to keep the price of a commodity or service low.
20. Technology Business Incubators (TBIs):  Incubating facility in an institution of higher learning and 
provides services such as business advice, financial counseling, assistance with business management 
and accounting, legal and regulatory guidance, access to mentors etc., in addition to physical 
infrastructure appropriate for the thrust area for the incubator.
21. Venture Capital: Venture Capital firms invest in startups at a later stage. Startups with a potential to 
grow, need a certain amount of investment. Wealthy investors like to invest their capital in such 
businesses with a long-term growth perspective. This capital is known as venture capital and the 
investors are called venture capitalists.
22. Venture Capital Funds: Investment funds that manage money from investors seeking equity stakes in 
startups with robust growth potential. These investments are characterized as high-risk/high-return 
opportunities.

other benefits such as government sponsored incubation support, acceleration support, capacity 
development workshops, mentorship and so on.
